# ML Affinity Scorer — replacing the failed docking score

Date: 2026-06-28 · Related: docking-benchmark log (docking enrichment ROC-AUC 0.455)
Script: `src/scoring/train_affinity_model.py` · Models: `models/*.joblib` (gitignored, reproducible)

## Why
The decoy-enrichment test proved smina/Vina docking score cannot rank FKBP12 binders (ROC-AUC 0.455). A ligand-based ML scorer was built to replace it for ranking/selection.

## Method
- Features: ECFP4 (Morgan r=2, 2048 bits).
- Model: RandomForest (classifier for triage; regressor for potency).
- Data: 725 ChEMBL FKBP12 binders (positives) + 1200 random drug-like ChEMBL molecules (negatives).
- **Leakage control:** the 60 enrichment molecules (20 actives + 40 decoys) are excluded from training and used only as an external test.
- Validation: random-split CV, **scaffold-split CV** (Murcko; tests new-chemotype generalization), external enrichment test, and a Tanimoto similarity audit.

## Results
| Task | Metric | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Binder triage | random-split CV ROC-AUC | 0.987 |
| Binder triage | **scaffold-split CV ROC-AUC** | **0.993** |
| Binder triage | external enrichment ROC-AUC | 1.000 (docking: 0.455) |
| Potency ranking | **scaffold-split Spearman ρ** | **0.748** (deterministic) |
| Potency ranking | R² / MAE | 0.56 / ~0.66 log units |

## Honest interpretation (what is and isn't true)
- **Real & robust:** the classifier separates FKBP12-binder chemical space from generic drug-like space even when whole scaffolds are held out (0.993) — not mere memorization. The regressor ranks potency across novel scaffolds (ρ 0.76, MAE ~0.66 log = ~4–5× in Ki), which is genuinely useful for lead ranking.
- **Over-stated by the enrichment test:** the external ROC-AUC 1.000 is on an EASY split — similarity audit shows enrichment actives sit at median Tanimoto 0.75 to training positives vs decoys at 0.21. So 1.000 reflects "near FKBP12 space vs random space," not fine discrimination. **The honest headline number is the scaffold-split 0.993 (triage) and ρ 0.76 (potency).**
- **Not addressed (the actual hard part):** SELECTIVITY vs calcineurin and FKBP12.6 — no counter-target data collected or modeled yet. This is the real differentiator and remains open.
- **Applicability domain:** predictions for de novo molecules far from training space (low max-Tanimoto) are unreliable; an AD/Tanimoto gate must accompany any use on generated molecules, or the generator will be rewarded for drifting out of domain.

## Re-verification (2026-06-28, round 2)
- **Classifier is FKBP-FAMILY, not FKBP12-specific.** Mean P(binder): FKBP12 actives 0.99, **FKBP5 ligands 0.79**, FKBP4 0.53, random ChEMBL 0.02. So it detects "FKBP-pocket-binder chemotype vs random," which is what triage needs — but it carries NO FKBP12 specificity. Docs/scorer updated to say so.
- **Selectivity-unpredictable conclusion is robust, not an artifact.** The standalone FKBP5 affinity regressor is itself good (scaffold-CV ρ 0.78, R² 0.61); yet FKBP12−FKBP5 selectivity prediction is ρ≈0. Two good models whose *difference* is unlearnable → selectivity genuinely not ligand-ML-solvable.
- **Reproducibility:** potency regressor is deterministic after fixing a `set()`-ordering bug in scaffold grouping (now `sorted(set(...))`); both runs ρ=0.748, R²=0.562. (Headline ρ stable ~0.75 across runs/splits.)
- FKBP4 regressor is weak (n=101, ρ 0.30) — note, secondary off-target.

## Decision
- **Adopt** the classifier+regressor as the Phase-2 ranking signal (replacing docking score), gated by the enrichment protocol (≥0.7) — passed.
- Docking retained for POSE generation only.
- **Required next:** (a) collect calcineurin (CHEMBL) + FKBP12.6 ligand data and build selectivity models; (b) implement an applicability-domain filter; (c) only then wire scoring into the generative loop.
